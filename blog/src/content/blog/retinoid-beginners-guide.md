Retinoids have the strongest evidence base of any over-the-counter ingredient for texture, fine lines and acne. They also have the highest quit rate — almost always because of how people start.

## The strengths, weakest to strongest

Retinoids are a family, not a single ingredient. Roughly in order of potency:

| Form | Availability | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retinyl esters | OTC | Gentlest, slowest results |
| Retinol | OTC | The common starting point |
| Retinaldehyde | OTC | Stronger, one step from active form |
| Adapalene | OTC in many countries | Best evidence for acne |
| Tretinoin | Prescription | Strongest evidence overall |

Each step needs conversion inside the skin to become active. Fewer conversion steps means faster results — and more irritation.

## Start lower and less often than feels necessary

The mistake is nightly use from day one. Try this instead:

- **Weeks 1–2:** twice a week, at night only
- **Weeks 3–4:** every third night
- **Weeks 5–8:** every other night
- **Beyond:** nightly *only if* your skin is comfortable

If you get flaking or tightness, drop back a step for two weeks. This is not a race, and there is no bonus for reaching nightly use.

## The sandwich method

If you're prone to irritation, buffer it:

1. Thin layer of moisturiser
2. Pea-sized amount of retinoid for the whole face
3. Moisturiser again

This slows absorption. You lose a little potency and avoid a lot of misery — a trade worth making if the alternative is quitting.

## What to pause while you adjust

For the first month, stop using exfoliating acids (AHAs/BHAs), scrubs, and anything labelled "resurfacing" on retinoid nights. Stacking actives is the fastest route to a damaged barrier.

## Sunscreen is not optional here

Retinoids thin the outermost layer of dead cells while thickening living skin underneath. The net effect is real photosensitivity. Daily SPF 30+ isn't a general wellness suggestion in this context — it's what keeps you from undoing the work.

## The purge, and how to tell it apart

Around **weeks 2–6** you may break out more. If spots appear where you normally break out and clear faster than usual, that's purging — accelerated turnover pushing existing congestion to the surface.

If you're breaking out in *new* areas, or you have burning, swelling or persistent stinging, that's irritation or a reaction, not purging. Stop and reassess.

Give it **12 weeks** before deciding whether it's working.
