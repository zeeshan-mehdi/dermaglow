If you only fix one thing about your routine, fix this one. Not because sunscreen is glamorous, but because the gap between how it's tested and how it's used is enormous.

## The number on the bottle is a laboratory number

SPF is measured at **2 mg per square centimetre** of skin. For an adult face and neck that works out to roughly **a third of a teaspoon** — or the "two finger" measurement: a strip squeezed along the length of your index and middle fingers.

Most people apply between a quarter and half of that. And protection doesn't fall linearly. Apply half the required amount of an SPF 50 and you're not getting SPF 25 — you're getting closer to SPF 7.

**This is why an SPF 50 applied thinly can underperform an SPF 30 applied properly.** The best sunscreen is the one you'll use generously.

## Reapplication, realistically

The standard advice is every two hours. That's correct for a beach day and unrealistic for an office day.

A more honest framing:

- **Indoors, away from windows:** one morning application is broadly fine
- **Near a window all day:** reapply once around midday — UVA passes through glass
- **Outdoors, commuting, or sport:** every two hours, no negotiation
- **Sweating or swimming:** immediately after towelling off

## Mineral or chemical?

Both work. The choice is about tolerance and feel, not safety.

- **Mineral** (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) sits on the surface. Despite the "physical blocker" label, these filters mostly *absorb* UV and convert it to heat — measurements put reflection and scattering at only around 5% of the work. Better tolerated by reactive skin and rosacea; can leave a white cast on deeper skin tones.
- **Chemical** (avobenzone, octocrylene and similar) also absorbs UV and converts it to heat. Cosmetically lighter, no cast, occasionally stings around the eyes.

So the real difference is filter chemistry and how each feels on your face — not "blocks" versus "absorbs."

Modern hybrid formulas blur this line considerably.

## Look for broad spectrum

SPF only rates UVB — the burning wavelength. UVA drives ageing and pigmentation, and passes through cloud and glass year-round. Check for **"broad spectrum"**, a **PA+++ / PA++++** rating, or the circled **UVA** mark used in Europe.

## The bits everyone misses

Ears, the sides of the neck, the back of the neck, eyelids, lips, and the backs of your hands. Dermatologists see a disproportionate number of skin cancers on ears and the left side of the face — the driver's side.

Sunscreen is the only product in a routine with genuinely preventative evidence behind it. Everything else is correction.
